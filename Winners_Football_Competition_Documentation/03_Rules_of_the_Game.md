# 03 --- Rules of the Game: Monkey Post 5-a-Side

**Important:** These rules capture the organizer's confirmed format.
Items marked **\[TO CONFIRM\]** must be settled and circulated before
the first match. The competition should not rely on assumptions about
conventional futsal or football where the local Monkey Post format
differs.

## 1. Teams and players

1.  Each team registers seven players.
2.  Five players from each team participate at a time.
3.  All five players may move freely around the playing area.
4.  There is no designated goalkeeper and no fixed post defender.
5.  No player may use their hands to keep, catch, hold or deliberately
    stop the ball.
6.  The precise hand/arm infringement standard, including accidental
    contact, is **\[TO CONFIRM\]**.

## 2. Substitutions

1.  Each team may make a maximum of two substitutions per match.
2.  Substitutions are permanent.
3.  A substituted player cannot return to that match.
4.  The referee must be informed and authorize the substitution.
5.  The substitution procedure and permitted stoppage are **\[TO
    CONFIRM\]**.

## 3. Match duration

1.  Regulation time consists of two halves of 20 minutes each.
2.  Half-time duration is **\[TO CONFIRM\]**.
3.  The method of timekeeping, whether the clock stops for injuries or
    other delays, and the interval between matches are **\[TO
    CONFIRM\]**.

## 4. Knockout matches

1.  A match tied at the end of regulation time proceeds to five minutes
    of extra time.
2.  If still tied, the match is decided by penalty kicks.
3.  Whether the five minutes is continuous or divided into periods is
    **\[TO CONFIRM\]**.
4.  Penalty kicks are direct kicks from a designated long-distance
    penalty spot toward the small goal.
5.  The exact penalty distance, number of initial kicks, eligible
    kickers, order and sudden-death procedure must be confirmed before
    the first knockout match.

## 5. Goals and goal area

1.  Matches use the competition's small Monkey Post goalposts.
2.  Goal dimensions and the exact conditions for a valid goal are **\[TO
    CONFIRM\]**.
3.  The organizer must clearly mark or identify the goal line and
    playing boundaries before matches begin.
4.  No player is assigned a goalkeeper role.
5.  The rules governing players standing in or around the goal/post
    area, and any restrictions on blocking a shot, are **\[TO
    CONFIRM\]**.

## 6. Fouls and restarts

The following must be published before the tournament: - What
constitutes a foul and dangerous play. - Whether slide tackles are
permitted. - Whether kick-ins, kick-offs or another restart method is
used. - How the ball is returned to play after leaving the boundary. -
Whether there is offside (recommended: no offside for this format,
subject to organizer approval). - The distance opponents must stand from
a restart. - Whether direct or indirect free kicks apply.

## 7. Cards

1.  Yellow card: ₦1,000 fine.
2.  Red card: ₦3,000 fine and automatic suspension from the next match.
3.  A player shown a red card must leave the playing area and may not
    continue participating in that match.
4.  Whether a dismissed player may be replaced, and the effect on team
    numbers, is **\[TO CONFIRM\]**.
5.  Whether two yellow cards in one match result in a red card, and
    whether yellow cards accumulate across matches, must be confirmed in
    the Disciplinary Policy.

## 8. Referee

1.  A referee shall be appointed for each match.
2.  The referee manages the match, records goals and cards, controls
    restarts and reports incidents.
3.  The referee may request phone footage review under the Video Review
    Guidelines.
4.  The referee must not personally collect card fines.

## 9. Match abandonment and safety

1.  The referee may stop or suspend a match where safety, serious
    misconduct, weather or another material issue requires it.
2.  The Match Coordinator shall record the reason and time.
3.  The Committee shall decide whether the match is resumed, replayed,
    awarded or otherwise resolved under a published policy.
4.  A first-aid and emergency response arrangement must be in place
    before competition begins.

## 10. Pre-match inspection

Before kickoff, the referee or match coordinator should check: - Playing
surface and boundaries. - Goalposts are stable and safe. - Ball is
suitable and in safe condition. - Teams have only registered eligible
players. - Players are wearing suitable footwear and clothing. -
First-aid and emergency arrangements are available.

## 11. Final pre-launch rule checklist

The organizer must approve: ball type/size; pitch dimensions; goal
dimensions; penalty distance; half-time length; clock rules; handball
definition; fouls and restarts; goal validity; post-area blocking;
penalty shootout procedure; red-card replacement; and match abandonment
procedure.
